\section{Method}

Fig.~\ref{fig:concept} shows the whole problem in one drawing. The robot follows the wall on its right, and sees as far as the scan
reaches. It reads three distances, works out how far off 45\,cm it will be
after driving on a bit, and turns by that much. Then the next scan arrives and
it does the same again, about 30 times a second.

\subsection{Task 1}
We calculated the inertia for each link. We took the shape and the size
from the collision tag. Our wheel also had the same
numbers as the example in the assignment text. That told us the masses and the
dimensions were correct. 

\smallskip
\textbf{\texttt{base\_link}}, cylinder, \mbox{$r=0.16$}, \mbox{$h=0.06$},
\mbox{$m=5.0$\,kg}:
\begin{align*}
I_{xx}=I_{yy} &= \frac{1}{12}m(3r^2+h^2) \\
              &= \frac{1}{12}(5.0)(0.0804) \\
              &= 3.35\times10^{-2}~\mathrm{kg\,m^2} \\[4pt]
I_{zz}        &= \frac{1}{2}mr^2 \\
              &= \frac{1}{2}(5.0)(0.0256) \\
              &= 6.40\times10^{-2}~\mathrm{kg\,m^2}
\end{align*}

\smallskip
\textbf{\texttt{left\_wheel\_link}} and \textbf{\texttt{right\_wheel\_link}},
cylinder, \mbox{$r=0.03$}, \mbox{$w=0.025$}, \mbox{$m=0.2$\,kg} each:
\begin{align*}
I_{xx}=I_{yy} &= \frac{1}{12}m(3r^2+w^2) \\
              &= \frac{1}{12}(0.2)(0.003325) \\
              &= 5.54\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{kg\,m^2} \\[4pt]
I_{zz}        &= \frac{1}{2}mr^2 \\
              &= \frac{1}{2}(0.2)(0.0009) \\
              &= 9.00\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{kg\,m^2}
\end{align*}

\smallskip
\textbf{\texttt{caster\_wheel\_link}}, sphere, \mbox{$r=0.02$},
\mbox{$m=0.1$\,kg}:
\begin{align*}
I_{xx}=I_{yy}=I_{zz} &= \frac{2}{5}mr^2 \\
                     &= \frac{2}{5}(0.1)(0.0004) \\
                     &= 1.60\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{kg\,m^2}
\end{align*}

\smallskip
\textbf{\texttt{lidar\_link}}, cylinder, \mbox{$r=0.03$},
\mbox{$h=0.01$}, \mbox{$m=0.1$\,kg}:
\begin{align*}
I_{xx}=I_{yy} &= \frac{1}{12}m(3r^2+h^2) \\
              &= \frac{1}{12}(0.1)(0.0028) \\
              &= 2.33\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{kg\,m^2} \\[4pt]
I_{zz}        &= \frac{1}{2}mr^2 \\
              &= \frac{1}{2}(0.1)(0.0009) \\
              &= 4.50\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{kg\,m^2}
\end{align*}



Our wheel is turned by its joint, not inside the link. So in the wheel link's own frame the round axis is still $z$, and the symmetry term stays on $I_{zz}$. Had we rotated the geometry inside the link, it would have moved to $I_{yy}$.

\subsection{Task 2}
Before we wrote any code, we sketched out the controller, as we can see in
Fig.~\ref{fig:t2_controller}. In our sketch we have two beams facing the wall
and one that goes straight ahead. The two side beams give us the angle of the
wall. Based on this, we can work out how far from the wall the robot will be
after driving forward a short way. We compare that to the distance we want to
hold. The bigger the gap, the harder the robot turns. We decided to hold 45\,cm from the wall. The nose sweeps a circle of 19\,cm when the robot spins, so this leaves a margin of 26\,cm. We wanted more than twice the sweep, so that we had a good margin for the robot to turn anywhere
along the wall without hitting it.

We ask the scan for an angle, and work out which ray that is from the message
itself, as we can see in Fig.~\ref{lst:t2_scan}. If the scan is narrow there is
no ray at $-90^\circ$, so we take the closest one it has. We also throw away
the rays that come back as \texttt{inf}. For each angle we take the shortest
reading in a small sector, so one bad ray cannot steer the robot.

%TC:ignore
\begin{figure}[H]
\begin{minted}{python}
@staticmethod
def index(scan, angle):
    i = int(round((angle - scan.angle_min) / scan.angle_increment))
    return max(0, min(len(scan.ranges) - 1, i))

def look(self, scan, angle):
    half = math.radians(self.p("sector"))
    lo = self.index(scan, angle - half)
    hi = self.index(scan, angle + half)
    good = [r for r in scan.ranges[lo:hi + 1]
            if scan.range_min < r < scan.range_max]
    return min(good) if good else scan.range_max
\end{minted}

\subsection{Task 4}

We spawned the robot at zero and let it drive for about 64 seconds, which is
around two laps. Then we froze it and read \texttt{/odom} and the Gazebo pose,
as we can see in Fig.~\ref{fig:t4_r1_odom}, Fig.~\ref{fig:t4_r1_gz} and
Fig.~\ref{fig:t4_r2}. We did this twice. The odometry gives the heading as
four numbers, not an angle. We turned it into a yaw with
\begin{align*}
\text{yaw} = 2\operatorname{atan2}(q_z, q_w)
\end{align*}
Table~\ref{tab:t4_drift} shows both runs.

The robot was 4.16\,m out of place in the first run and 4.73\,m out in the
second, and its heading was off by 22 and 32 degrees. The plugin builds the
odometry from the wheel radius $r$, the separation $L$ and the wheel speeds:
\begin{align*}
v = \frac{r(\omega_R + \omega_L)}{2}, \qquad
\omega = \frac{r(\omega_R - \omega_L)}{L}
\end{align*}
Our $r$ and $L$ come from the same Xacro properties the wheels are built from,
so the error is in the wheel speeds. The wheels turn more than the robot moves.
The error went the same way in both runs. The robot turns the same way at
every corner, so the errors add up instead of cancelling out.

\section{Reflection}

For Task 2, we had a fun time debugging the lidar scanner, as the scan was
lagging behind the robot when it was driving, as we can see in
Fig.~\ref{fig:t2_bug}. We used over two hours debugging this and tried a lot of
different things, but nothing fixed it properly. First we tried to drop the
scan from 30 to 10\,Hz. That helped the scan keep up, but the robot crashed in
the corners as it had fewer scans to turn on. Finally, we got a tip from Claude
that we could make the Gazebo window smaller. We tried it, and it just worked,
and the scan was now following the robot, as we can see in
Fig.~\ref{fig:t2_fix}. It turned out that the GPU cost scales with the pixel
count, so fewer pixels made the GPU work less, and that was enough for the scan
to keep up with the robot.

For Task 3, we had some problems with getting the robot to work with the
120 degree scan, as the robot just turned around when it hit the wall and
could never finish the maze. We spent a lot of time trying to figure
out what the error was before we could figure out how to fix it. The
scan had no rays straight out to the side, so the second ray
read the wall in front instead of the wall to the right.
We fixed this as we showed in the results section by making the gaps
between the two rays follow the scan.

For Task 4 we did not have any big issues.

For the recording of the
video, we also had some problems. We first tried to use a program
called Peek to record, but this gave us a laggy recording as it used
a lot of GPU space. We then figured out that we could use the Gazebo
built-in recorder, and this worked a lot better, and it was less laggy.
